Стр. 410 · (начало фрагмента)

It is important for proper subsurface correlations to understand that clinoforms, which represent geological time lines, crosscut lithostratigraphic boundaries. The hypothetical example in Fig. 9.10 shows correlations based upon lithofacies (stratigraphic intervals with similarities in well log response) and upon geological time. Note that shaley and heterolithic intervals separate sandy intervals, so that the sands between wells 1 and 2 are not in communication. This feature will adversely affect waterflood performance as well as single-well productivity.

Стр. 410 · (начало фрагмента)

A similar example is presented for the modern Mekong River Delta in Fig. 9.11 (after Gani and Bhattacharya, 2005). Progradation of the delta has been age-dated for the last 6000 years. Lithostratigraphic and sequence stratigraphic (chronostratigraphic) correlations are given to illustrate the crossing nature of time lines and lithofacies boundaries. The true chronostratigraphic correlations demonstrate the potential for lateral discontinuities within the lithostratigraphic intervals.

Стр. 410 · (начало фрагмента)

In addition to sea-level fluctuations, deltas are subject to “delta distributary switching processes,” such as modeled in Fig. 9.12. According to Overeem et al. (2005), channel switching may be an even more important control on the evolution of deltaic systems than is sea-level change.

Стр. 410 · (начало фрагмента)

Deltas can also form during a relative fall in sea level; these are often referred to as “lowstand” or “shelf-margin” deltas or systems tracts (Figs. 5.18 and 5.19). These deposits form in response to early fall in sea level, which causes valley incision, and a seaward retreat and lowering of the shoreline. Although important, these lowstand deltas are generally not as large, nor as prevalent as highstand deltas.

Стр. 411 · 9.4.1 Processes and Deposits

The Mississippi River Delta (Fig. 9.13) has long been considered the “type” river-dominated delta (Fig. 9.5). Strong waves and currents do not impinge upon its protected shoreline, so sediment deposited at and near the shore zone is not reworked or dispersed laterally (Figs. 9.4 and 9.7). With time and sufficient accommodation space between the sea surface and seafloor to accept sediment, such a delta will prograde seaward (Fig. 9.9), as will the delta zones (Fig. 9.7). By that process, surfaces representing instants in geologic time crosscut boundaries between sedimentary facies (see the time lines or clinoforms in Fig. 9.9). However, over time, progradation and vertical aggradation lead to a progressive shallowing of the water depth, to the point at which the main river(s) feeding sediment to its delta will shift toward deeper water (toward lower points on the seafloor), thereby causing the distributary-channel switching discussed

Стр. 411 · 9.4.1 Processes and Deposits

The main facies of a river-dominated delta, and their distribution in space, are shown in Fig. 9.14. Sandy reservoir facies are deposits of distributary channels and distributary mouth-bars. Interdistributary bays, marshes, and lagoons separate sandy facies and provide shale barriers in subsurface reservoirs, and sometimes, they provide hydrocarbon source rocks. The different facies can be recognized in outcrop by facies relationships and by a distinctive coarsening-upward grain size with a characteristic assemblage of sedimentary structures (Fig. 9.15). In the subsurface, these same features can be identified in cores and on well logs, including a lateral thinning and fining log response away from the channel axis (Fig. 9.15).

Стр. 415 · 9.4.1 Processes and Deposits

In Prudhoe Bay field, formative processes of distributary mouth bars were evaluated by Tye and Hickey (2001). A 1000-ft. (300-m) horizontal well was drilled into a distributary mouth-bar reservoir sandstone, and three cores were obtained. Seven sandy lithofacies were identified in the core, all are moderately to very well sorted and fine to very fine-grained. Six of the lithofacies exhibit average horizontal permeabilities of 12–40 md. The seventh, best-sorted lithofacies averages 129 md. The origin of these lithofacies was determined by studying modern river-dominated delta processes and sediments. Geoscientists believe that sediment plumes emanating from distributary channels during flood stage deposited the best-sorted and most-permeable sands (the seventh lithofacies) at the apex or most-proximal part of the distributary mouth bars, as well as along the proximal-bar margin (Fig. 9.16). Finer-grained, more poorly sorted sands and

Стр. 416 · 9.4.2 Reservoir Example: Prudhoe Bay Field

General geology of Prudhoe Bay field, Alaska, in the river-dominated deltaic deposits of the ancient Sagavanirktok River, was discussed in Chapter 7. The most prolific reservoir rocks in the field are of fluvial, braided-river origin. The lower stratigraphic interval, called the Romeo Zone (Figs. 9.16–9.18), is composed mainly of river-dominated delta-front and prodelta deposits (Fig. 7.22 and 7.23, and Fig. 9.17) that in recent years have been targeted for production, mainly from horizontal wells (Tye and Hickey, 2001; Tye et al., 1999). Productive deltaic facies are distributary-channel and distributary mouth-bar sandstones (Figs. 9.18 and 9.19). The depositional history depicts southeastward progradation of low-gradient, fine-grained fluvial systems that extended about 60 miles (100 km) from their source area. Individual fluvial channels were separated laterally by muddy floodplains. In the down-current direction, fluvial channels

River-dominated deposition resulted in significant lateral and vertical facies changes, which caused a high degree of lateral and vertical heterogeneity in the resulting reservoir. Past reservoir characterization studies of this delta complex had correlated sandstone and shale units on the basis of lithostratigraphic features. However, Tye et al. (1999) were better able to match well correlations with production history using a sequence- or time-stratigraphic correlation scheme (Fig. 9.18). Prior to this sequence stratigraphic interpretation, the gently dipping nature of these reservoir sandstones was unrecognized and led to improper correlations.

Стр. 417 · 9.4.2 Reservoir Example: Prudhoe Bay Field

Insights gained into the formation of distributary mouth bars have led to improved recovery in the Prudhoe Bay field. Detailed information on the lateral attributes of the mouth bars (including permeability), gained from the horizontal, cored well rather than from interpretations of standard vertical cores, provided the knowledge that a sinusoidal, horizontal well path would contact more high-permeability lithofacies than would a conventional well.

Стр. 417 · 9.4.2 Reservoir Example: Prudhoe Bay Field

Shales also were found to be particularly important to production and fluid flow in Prudhoe Bay field's deltaic facies. Some shales are continuous over areas larger than that of the well spacing, but other shales are less continuous because of erosion by an overlying sandstone or by stratigraphic pinch-out (Fig. 9.20). Continuous shales create vertical flow barriers and help to focus a waterflood. By restricting perforations in horizontal wells to deltaic sandstones, developers avoided competition for water between these lower-permeability sandstones and overlying higher-permeability fluvial sandstones (Figs. 9.21 and 9.22) (Tye et al., 1999). One innovative application of the understanding of shale continuity was applied to a vertical well that had maintained a steady output in excess of 300 BOPD between May 1992 and January 1993. During that time, water production increased, indicating that oil production in that well would decline

considerably over time (Fig. 9.23). A shale interval at 8950 ft. was mapped as a sealing shale, which suggests that oil might be trapped in an underlying deltaic sandstone. A sidetrack horizontal well was drilled as an unconventional completion into the sandstone, and it led to new production of approximately 400 BOPD (Fig. 9.23).

Стр. 419 · 9.4.2 Reservoir Example: Prudhoe Bay Field

A second example of the value of understanding shale continuity is presented in Fig. 9.24. The horizontal well 11-30 was drilled beneath a bay shale that acted as a shield from the overlying gas cap. 894 ft. (272 m)-long horizontal section was drilled entirely within the targeted sandstone, of which 475 ft. (144 m) was determined to be net pay. As of 1999, production had stabilized at 1600 BOPD, with a low gas/oil ratio (Tye et al., 1999). Detailed characterizations and applications such as these are increasing the life of this giant field.

Стр. 422 · 9.4.3 Kuparuk River Field, Alaska USA

A similar example is provided by the Cretaceous Kuparuk River field on the North Slope of Alaska. This is the second largest oil field in North America, with 6BBSTOOIP. As of 2006, 2BBO had been produced. Production is from two intervals, named the A and C units. In the early days of field development each unit was considered a laterally continuous sandstone body. However, sequence stratigraphy correlations revealed that the A unit consists of six separate sandstone bodies that prograded in the seaward (northeast) direction, each separated by a high-frequency transgressive marine shale (Fig. 9.25).
